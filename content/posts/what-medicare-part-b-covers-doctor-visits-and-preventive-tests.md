Medicare can feel like a maze, and one of the most common questions is simple and urgent: what does Medicare Part B cover when you visit the doctor or need preventive tests? That question matters because the difference between a covered screening and an unexpected bill can be hundreds or thousands of dollars. Understanding what Part B pays for, and how costs are calculated, makes it easier to get the care you need without surprises.

This article explains, in clear terms, what Medicare Part B covers for doctor visits and preventive tests. You will find specific examples, practical steps to reduce out-of-pocket costs, and the distinctions between routine exams, preventive screenings, and diagnostic services that follow a symptom.

What does Medicare Part B cover: the big picture

Medicare Part B handles outpatient medical care. That includes most doctor visits, outpatient procedures, diagnostic tests such as lab work and imaging, certain preventive screenings, and durable medical equipment. Generally, for most services after you meet the annual Part B deductible, Medicare pays 80 percent of the Medicare-approved amount and you pay the remaining 20 percent, unless the service is covered fully as a preventive benefit. To avoid balance billing or higher patient responsibility, confirm that your doctor accepts Medicare assignment before you receive the service.

Part B is not designed to pay for long-term custodial care, routine dental care, hearing aids, or most vision services. Those gaps mean that depending on your health needs you might consider Medigap or a Medicare Advantage plan to fill charges Part B does not cover.

Doctor visits covered by Part B

Primary care and specialist visits: Office visits to your primary care physician and to specialists are core Part B benefits. That includes visits for new symptoms, management of chronic conditions, medication adjustments, and many outpatient consultations. If a doctor orders tests during the visit, Medicare will typically also cover those tests subject to deductible and coinsurance rules.

Annual Wellness Visit and initial preventive physical exam: Medicare covers an Annual Wellness Visit to create or update a personalized prevention plan. This visit focuses on risk factors, health screenings, and creating a plan to prevent disease. It is different from a comprehensive physical exam, which may include services not fully covered as preventive, and those may trigger cost sharing. The Initial Preventive Physical Examination, sometimes called the Welcome to Medicare preventive visit, is covered within the first 12 months of Medicare Part B enrollment.

Mental health and counseling: Outpatient mental health visits to psychiatrists, psychologists, and qualified therapists are covered by Part B when they are medically necessary or part of behavioral health treatment. Telehealth visits for mental health are also included when provided by a Medicare-approved practitioner and billed correctly.

Telehealth and virtual visits: Telehealth coverage has expanded. Part B covers video visits with your doctor for many conditions when the provider bills Medicare and follows the program’s rules. Some telehealth services may require the same cost sharing as in-person visits.

Ambulance and urgent outpatient services: Medically necessary ambulance transport to the nearest appropriate facility is typically covered by Part B. Urgent outpatient procedures and same-day surgery performed in an ambulatory surgical center are also covered when physician-approved and billed to Medicare.

Preventive tests Part B covers and how often

Part B includes a broad set of preventive services, many of which are covered at no cost when you use a Medicare-enrolled provider that accepts assignment. Preventive tests are intended to detect disease early or identify risk factors so treatment can begin sooner. Below are the most common tests and what you should know about each.

Annual Wellness Visit: Covered once every 12 months. This visit is not a physical exam in the traditional sense. It focuses on health risk assessment, preventive screening schedules, and a written plan to prevent illness.

Flu, pneumonia, and COVID-19 vaccines: Seasonal influenza shots, pneumococcal vaccines, and COVID-19 vaccines are covered under Part B. Coverage includes administration of the vaccine, and there is typically no coinsurance when the provider accepts assignment.

Cancer screenings: Mammograms for women are covered annually for screening purposes, with no coinsurance when billed as a screening. Colon cancer screening is covered in several forms, including screening colonoscopy, fecal occult blood testing, and certain lab-based stool DNA tests under specific frequency rules. If a screening colonoscopy becomes diagnostic because a polyp is removed, coinsurance and deductible may apply for the associated procedure.

Diabetes screenings and supplies: Medicare covers diabetes screenings when you meet certain risk factors or have been referred by a physician, and covers diabetes self-management training, some supplies, and certain glucose monitors when they meet Medicare criteria. Continuous glucose monitor coverage depends on the type and clinical need.

Bone mass measurement: Bone density tests are covered for people at risk for osteoporosis, generally every 24 months or more often if medically necessary.

Cardiovascular disease screenings: Routine blood tests for cholesterol, lipid, and triglyceride levels are covered every five years. Medicare also covers electrocardiograms and other cardiovascular diagnostic services when they are ordered by a doctor to evaluate or monitor symptoms or conditions.

Lung cancer screening: Low-dose CT scan for lung cancer screening can be covered for people who meet specific criteria, typically age and smoking history requirements, and who receive a referral from a doctor.

Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening: A one-time ultrasound is covered for men who have ever smoked and who are in the eligible age range, usually between 65 and 75 years, when ordered by a doctor.

Prostate cancer screening: Medicare covers prostate-specific antigen, or PSA, tests and digital rectal exams for men under Medicare who meet coverage criteria. Frequency and coverage may be updated by preventive guidelines, so check current recommendations with your provider.

Depression and cognitive impairment screenings: Screening for depression and for cognitive impairment is covered when performed in a primary care setting and when appropriate follow-up is available.

Diagnostic tests, lab work and outpatient procedures

The distinction between preventive screening and a diagnostic test matters for costs. Screening tests performed as part of preventive care are often covered without coinsurance. However, if a screening becomes diagnostic because you develop symptoms, or a provider detects an issue during a screening and orders additional tests or treatment, those subsequent services are typically billed as diagnostic and subject to deductible and coinsurance.

Common outpatient diagnostic services covered under Part B include blood tests, urinalysis, X-rays, CT scans, MRIs, EKGs, and biopsy procedures ordered by a physician. If a test is medically necessary and ordered by a Medicare-enrolled provider, Part B generally covers it, but you should expect cost sharing unless the test remains in the preventive category.

Durable medical equipment and supplies

Part B covers certain durable medical equipment, often abbreviated DME, that your doctor prescribes for use at home. Examples include walkers, wheelchairs, oxygen equipment and supplies, and some hospital beds when medically necessary. Medicare will cover DME that meets specific medical criteria, and you will usually pay 20 percent of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible, unless you have supplemental coverage.

Expectations and scenarios that clarify coverage

Scenario 1: Mr. Thompson has new chest pain and sees his primary care doctor. The doctor orders an EKG and a stress test. Because these services respond to a symptom rather than routine screening, Medicare covers them but Mr. Thompson is responsible for coinsurance and any deductible.

Scenario 2: Ms. Alvarez schedules her Annual Wellness Visit. During the visit the doctor orders a screening mammogram and a flu shot. The mammogram and the flu shot, billed correctly as preventive services, are covered with no coinsurance, assuming the provider accepts assignment.

Scenario 3: Mr. Jackson is a current smoker aged 68 with at least a 20 pack-year history. His physician refers him for a low-dose CT lung cancer screening. The scan is covered if he meets Medicare criteria and the provider follows Medicare rules for documentation and referral.

Costs, billing rules and how to avoid surprises

Medicare Part B has three basic cost components: the monthly Part B premium, an annual deductible, and coinsurance for most outpatient services after the deductible is met. Many preventive services are covered with no coinsurance when a provider accepts assignment. To reduce unexpected costs verify the following before receiving care.

Confirm that the provider accepts Medicare assignment, which limits what the doctor can bill you beyond Medicare-approved amounts. If a provider does not accept assignment, you may be subject to balance billing, which increases your out-of-pocket cost.

Ask whether a service will be billed as a preventive screening or as a diagnostic test if you have symptoms. The billing category affects whether you owe coinsurance.

If durable medical equipment is prescribed, ask about prior authorization requirements, and whether the supplier participates in Medicare. Some devices require documentation and prior approval to ensure coverage.

Coordinate coverage with supplemental plans. If you have a Medigap policy, it will often cover the 20 percent coinsurance and the Part B deductible. If you use a Medicare Advantage plan, check the plan’s prior authorization rules, network restrictions, and any copay schedules for specialist visits and tests.

Enrollment timing and additional coverage options

Timely enrollment in Part B avoids penalties and gaps. Most people enroll during an initial enrollment period around their 65th birthday, but special situations can change deadlines. If you delay Part B because of employer-sponsored coverage, confirm how that group insurance coordinates with Medicare and whether you will face a late enrollment penalty later.

Consider supplemental coverage for routine dental, hearing or vision needs, because Part B does not usually cover those services. Medicare Advantage plans often include bundled benefits for dental and vision, but they come with networks and different cost-sharing rules, so compare plans carefully.

Practical steps to make the most of Part B coverage

Before scheduling care, call your provider and confirm they accept Medicare. Ask how they will code a screening versus diagnostic test, and what documentation or referrals are needed. Use the Annual Wellness Visit to keep preventive screening schedules on track. If you have a chronic condition such as diabetes, maintain regular follow-up with your provider and verify which monitoring supplies and education services Medicare covers for your situation.

Keep records of referrals, test orders, and any pre-authorizations. If you receive a Medicare Summary Notice or a bill that looks incorrect, contact the provider and Medicare. Errors in coding happen, and correcting them can eliminate improper charges.

Final summary

Medicare Part B covers a wide range of doctor visits, preventive screenings, diagnostic tests, and medically necessary durable medical equipment. Preventive services such as the Annual Wellness Visit, certain vaccines, and many cancer screenings can be covered with no coinsurance when billed as preventive care by a provider who accepts assignment. For symptomatic problems and diagnostic workups, expect cost sharing after the Part B deductible unless you have supplemental coverage. Confirm providers accept Medicare, clarify whether a service will be billed as preventive or diagnostic, and use the Annual Wellness Visit to keep screening on schedule. These steps will help you get the care you need with fewer surprises and lower out-of-pocket costs.
